Annotated bibliography

Five sources used throughout this tutorial

[1] Funk et al., 2018 — PressNET

Full citation: Christopher Funk, Savinay Nagendra, Jesse Scott, Bharadwaj Ravichandran, John H. Challis, Robert T. Collins, and Yanxi Liu. 2018. Learning Dynamics from Kinematics: Estimating 2D Foot Pressure Maps from Video Frames. arXiv:1811.12607.

Synopsis: Introduces PressNET, a convolutional network that predicts a foot-pressure heatmap from a single frame of 2D body pose. Trains on six people performing Tai Chi, with synchronized video and insole pressure sensors as ground truth. Beats a K-nearest-neighbor baseline on pressure error and on center-of-pressure error, and shows its predictions stay smooth over time. This is the main source for the whole tutorial.

Peer-reviewed adjacentPrimary source

Reliability: High. Detailed methodology, real experimental data, and a named research group at Penn State, though the version used is an arXiv preprint rather than a published journal article.

[2] Scott et al., 2020 — kinematics to dynamics

Full citation: Jesse Scott, Christopher Funk, Bharadwaj Ravichandran, John H. Challis, Robert T. Collins, and Yanxi Liu. 2020. From Kinematics to Dynamics: Estimating Center of Pressure and Base of Support from Video Frames of Human Motion. arXiv:2001.00657.

Synopsis: A follow-up study from the same research group. Adds a larger dataset, a simpler fully connected network alongside PressNET, and extends the output from pressure alone to center of pressure and base of support. Used throughout this tutorial to confirm PressNET's results generalize and to introduce base of support.

Peer-reviewed adjacentPrimary source

Reliability: High. Same authors and lab as [1], larger dataset, and consistent methodology, though also an arXiv preprint.

[3] Morris et al., 2021 — ground reaction force in sidestepping

Full citation: Corey Morris, Marion Mundt, Molly Goldacre, Jason Weber, Ajmal Mian, and Jacqueline Alderson. 2021. Predicting 3D Ground Reaction Force from 2D Video via Neural Networks in Sidestepping Tasks. ISBS Proceedings Archive 39, 1 (2021), Article 77.

Synopsis: Applies the same pose-to-force idea directly to an athletic movement, sidestepping, instead of Tai Chi. Trains neural networks on 14 participants to predict 3D ground reaction force from 2D video pose, reaching correlation coefficients up to 0.954. Used in the Future section as evidence the same approach already extends to sports movement.

Peer-reviewed conference

Reliability: High. Published through the International Society of Biomechanics in Sports, a recognized sports-science conference.

[4] Louis et al., 2022 — external forces via transformer

Full citation: Nathan Louis, Jason J. Corso, Tylan N. Templin, Travis D. Eliason, and Daniel P. Nicolella. 2022. Learning to Estimate External Forces of Human Motion in Video. In Proceedings of the 30th ACM International Conference on Multimedia (ACMMM '22). ACM.

Synopsis: Frames ground-reaction-force estimation explicitly around sports performance and injury prevention, and is the first to use a transformer for this task instead of a CNN or LSTM. Also shows that pre-training on 2D-to-3D pose estimation improves how well the force model generalizes to new movements. Used in the Future section to show the field moving past PressNET's original architecture.

Peer-reviewed conference

Reliability: High. Published at ACM Multimedia, a major, competitive, peer-reviewed venue.

[5] Koleini et al., 2025 — BioPose

Full citation: Farnoosh Koleini, Muhammad Usama Saleem, Pu Wang, Hongfei Xue, Ahmed Helmy, and Abbey Fenwick. 2025. BioPose: Biomechanically-Accurate 3D Pose Estimation from Monocular Videos. In Proceedings of the IEEE/CVF Winter Conference on Applications of Computer Vision (WACV).

Synopsis: Improves the accuracy of the 3D pose estimation step itself by adding biomechanical corrections, rather than working on force or pressure prediction. Relevant because pose accuracy is an upstream bottleneck: any error in the joint positions carries forward into a system like PressNET. Used in the Challenges section to support the pose-accuracy limitation.

Peer-reviewed conference

Reliability: High. Published at WACV, a well-regarded IEEE/CVF computer vision conference.
